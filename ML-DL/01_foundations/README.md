# Part 1: Mathematical & Conceptual Foundations

A recap of the mathematical and conceptual background needed for the deep learning part of the course. These topics are usually covered in other courses, so this part is kept short; the focus of the course, including most exercises, is on [Part 2](../02_deep_learning/). The material is based on Bishop & Bishop (2024), Chapters 1–3 and the appendices, and on Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville (2016). Full references are in the [course README](../README.md).

| Notebook | Overview |
|---|---|
| [`00_notation.ipynb`](00_notation.ipynb) | Reference handout (not presented in class) listing the mathematical notation and the callouts used throughout the course. |
| [`01_Introduction.ipynb`](01_Introduction.ipynb) | Introduces machine learning and deep learning, covering the history of AI, its symbolic and subsymbolic paradigms, and the formal learning framework. |
| [`02_probability.ipynb`](02_probability.ipynb) | Represents uncertainty with probability theory, from Bayes' theorem and the Gaussian distribution to maximum likelihood, density transformations and MAP estimation. |
| [`03_information.ipynb`](03_information.ipynb) | Quantifies uncertainty with information theory: entropy, KL divergence, cross-entropy as a loss function, and mutual information. |
| [`04_algebra.ipynb`](04_algebra.ipynb) | Reviews the linear algebra behind deep learning, from vectors, norms and tensors to least squares, eigendecomposition, SVD and low-rank approximations. |
| [`05_functions.ipynb`](05_functions.ipynb) | Surveys activation and output functions, from step and sigmoid through the ReLU family to GELU, together with softmax and canonical link functions. |
| [`06_mlops.ipynb`](06_mlops.ipynb) | Introduces machine learning operations along the CRISP-ML(Q) process, from data and model engineering to evaluation, deployment and monitoring. |
